The problem

No open data on astronauts' bodies,
and no doctor 22 light-minutes away.

  • Crews beyond Earth orbit have up to ~22 minutes of one-way delay, no specialist, and too few people to average over.
  • Open per-person astronaut data barely exist: we found one study (four Inspiration4 crew, NASA OSDR).
  • So a monitor cannot be validated on the people it protects. Its best property is knowing what it cannot see.
  • ALMA: the patient is the person; the habitat is only an input to the body.

Receipts and hashes

Sealed before it ran.

  • 30 experiments hashed with SHA-256 before they run; 25 executed, each once.
  • Failures are published like successes; an "all clear" without a receipt does not appear.
  • Anyone can recompute every hash in the browser ("Verify all").
  • We report our own errors: a "10 of 10" result did not replicate (8 of 10).
